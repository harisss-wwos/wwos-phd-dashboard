About the PHD Program

The Potential Harm to Drivers (PHD) program is a security risk mitigation function within Worldwide Operations Security (WWOS), operating under the GSOC. It protects Last Mile delivery transporters from violence, threats, and harassment originating from customers or identifiable Persons of Interest (POI), focusing on reducing repeat incidents that could cause physical, mental, or emotional harm.

How it functions (the workflow)

  • Receives incident reports via EMT notifications and the LM-HUB ticketing system
  • Investigates using multiple evidence sources — video footage, route data, background checks, witness statements
  • Assesses risk using a severity framework (SEV1–SEV5)
  • Applies mitigations — address blocking, account actions, geo-routing updates, transporter support
  • Monitors repeat offenses, targeting a repeat known-POI offense rate below 1%
  • Coordinates with SDS, DSP Ops, Flex Safety, and Global Road Safety

Scope

  • In scope: violence/threats/harassment with harm potential, incidents from identifiable customers/POI, pet incidents (pet as weapon, EMS transport, repeats), and on-road detrimental behavior (assault, theft, impeding egress, intimidation, etc.)
  • Out of scope: unidentifiable POI, collisions/non-behavioral safety, package theft when transporter absent, vandalism where transporter isn't the direct victim

Guiding principles (tenets)

Transporter safety over package delivery; safety is transporter-agnostic; robust but flexible processes; prioritize risk frameworks over opinion; consider the totality of risk (location + POI history); evidence-driven, unbiased assessments.

Geographic coverage

  • AMER: Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, US
  • EMEA: EU, South Africa, UK
  • APAC: Australia, India, Japan, Singapore
